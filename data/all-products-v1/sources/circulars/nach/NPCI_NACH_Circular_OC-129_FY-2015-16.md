3. The banks have an option to retain the "on-us" transaction with them and not route
through NPCl. However, in such cases, the interchange of Rs. 0.25 per transaction will not
be payable to the destination bank.
4. NPCl will recover the aforesaid transaction charges from the sponsor bank of the
concerned ministry at monthly interval as hitherto and distribute the interchange fee to
the destination bank. The sponsor bank, in turn, will debit the relevant account of the
concerned ministry in the same scheme head from where re-imbursement of DBT is
obtained.

2. The issue of transaction charges on account of payments of Direct Benefit
Transfer (DBT) schemes has been examined vis-a-vis the feedback received from
31.07.2015, it has been decided that:
(i)All DBT transactions should be routed through the National Payment
be paid to the NPcl from the same budget line from which the respective
scheme funds/benefits are being made available.
(ii) For schemes like MGNREGA, old age pension, maternity benefits and school
level scholarships where disbursement in far-fiung areas is critical,an additional
disbursement incentive of 1%, subject to a minimum of Rs. 5/- and a maximum
of Rs.10/-, will be payable to the bank/post office handling the beneficiary
account from the same budget line from which the respective scheme
funds/benefits are being made available.The banks must ensure efficient last-
mile cash out facility for the beneficiaries through their Branch/ATiM/Banking
Correspondent Common Service Centres/ Mobile payment system etc.
Similarly. Department of Posts must ensure easy cash out facility through its
network.
